## Chapter 1. Terminology and the terminological field of the automobile industry (≈ 6–7 pages)

### 1.1 The notion of *term* and terminological system (≈ 2 pages)

**What to cover**
- Definition of *term* in modern linguistics; *term* vs *common word*.
- Core properties of a term: systemicity, monosemy within a field,
  motivatedness, stylistic neutrality, conventionality.
- Terminological field, terminological system, terminology as a
  discipline.
- Standardisation of terminology: national (ДСТУ) and international
  (ISO/TC 37).

**Sources to cite**
- Селіванова О. О. *Сучасна лінгвістика: термінологічна
  енциклопедія.*
- Д'яков А. С., Кияк Т. Р., Куделько З. Б. *Основи термінотворення.*
- Суперанская А. В., Подольская Н. В., Васильева Н. В. *Общая
  терминология: вопросы теории.*
- Felber H. *Terminology Manual* (UNESCO).
- Cabré M. T. *Terminology: Theory, Methods and Applications.*

### 1.2 The terminological field of the automobile industry (≈ 2 pages)

**What to cover**
- Place of automotive terminology within technical terminology.
- Subdomains of the field (powertrain, transmission, chassis, body,
  electrical, safety, maintenance, fuel, production, market) —
  matches the 13 thematic clusters in the practical glossary.
- Internal hierarchy: hyperonyms (vehicle) → hyponyms (passenger
  car, truck, SUV) → meronyms (engine → cylinder → piston).
- Synchronic dynamics of the field: electrification, driver
  assistance, telematics as sources of new terms.

**Sources to cite**
- ДСТУ 2960-94 *Транспорт автомобільний. Терміни та визначення.*
- Кочерган М. П. *Загальне мовознавство.*
- VDA (Verband der Automobilindustrie) terminological publications.
- SAE International glossaries.

### 1.3 Comparative aspects of automotive terminology in the UA-EN-DE triad (≈ 2–3 pages)

**What to cover**
- Equivalence types: full equivalents, partial equivalents,
  zero-equivalence (with descriptive translation).
- Borrowings: German → English (*Autobahn, Kindergarten*-style
  precedents → *Volkswagen, Kombi*), English → Ukrainian
  (*хетчбек, кросовер, тюнінг*), German → Ukrainian (*кардан,
  карбюратор, штрафбат*-era loans).
- Calquing: *повний привід* ← *all-wheel drive*; *подушка безпеки*
  ← *airbag* / *Luftsack*.
- Internationalisms and shared abbreviations: ABS, ESC/ESP, ECU,
  GPS, EV.
- Regional variation inside English: *hood / bonnet*, *trunk / boot*,
  *fender / wing*, *petrol / gasoline*, *tire / tyre* — and how it
  affects the choice of headword in the practical glossary.
- Lexico-semantic asymmetry: where one language has one term and
  another has two (e.g. EN *bearing* ↔ UA *підшипник* / *опора*).

**Sources to cite**
- Карабан В. І. *Переклад англійської наукової і технічної
  літератури.*
- Корунець І. В. *Теорія і практика перекладу.*
- Newmark P. *A Textbook of Translation.*
- Snell-Hornby M. *Translation Studies: An Integrated Approach.*

## Chapter 2. Multilingual lexicography and digital tools for linguistic work (≈ 5–7 pages)

### 2.1 Multilingual dictionaries: typology and structure (≈ 2 pages)

**What to cover**
- Classification of dictionaries: by language coverage
  (monolingual / bilingual / multilingual), by function (descriptive /
  prescriptive / pedagogical), by domain (general / specialised /
  terminological), by user (translator / learner / specialist).
- Macrostructure: word list, ordering principles, thematic vs
  alphabetical arrangement.
- Microstructure of an entry: headword, grammatical info, definition,
  equivalent(s), context, register marker, examples, cross-references
  — and how each maps to a column in the practical glossary
  (`Ukrainian`, `English`, `German`, `Notes`).
- The glossary as a sub-type: lighter microstructure, domain-bound,
  often bidirectional.

**Sources to cite**
- Дубічинський В. В. *Лексикографія.*
- Карпіловська Є. А. *Вступ до прикладної лінгвістики: комп'ютерна
  лінгвістика.*
- Hartmann R. R. K. & James G. *Dictionary of Lexicography.*
- Atkins B. T. S. & Rundell M. *The Oxford Guide to Practical
  Lexicography.*

### 2.2 From print to electronic lexicography (≈ 2 pages)

**What to cover**
- Historical shift: card files → printed dictionaries → CD-ROM →
  online → API-driven.
- Data formats for linguistic resources: XML/TEI, TBX (TermBase
  eXchange), JSON, CSV; trade-offs between human-readability,
  machine-readability and long-term preservation.
- Search affordances in electronic dictionaries: exact match,
  substring match, fuzzy / Levenshtein, lemmatised search,
  morphological expansion, full-text search across examples.
- Case-insensitivity and Unicode normalisation as a basic
  requirement for any multilingual resource — link this directly
  to the diacritic-stripping logic in the practical part.
- Examples of online multilingual terminological resources:
  *Multitran*, *Linguee*, *Glosbe*, *IATE* (the EU
  inter-institutional terminology base), *TermNet*.

**Sources to cite**
- Карпіловська Є. А. *Вступ до комп'ютерної лінгвістики.*
- Granger S. & Paquot M. (eds.) *Electronic Lexicography.*
- de Schryver G.-M. *Lexicographer's Dreams in the Electronic-
  Dictionary Age.*
- ISO 30042:2008 *Systems to manage terminology, knowledge and
  content — TermBase eXchange (TBX).*

### 2.3 Generative AI in lexicographic and terminological work (≈ 2–3 pages)

**What to cover**
- A brief history: rule-based MT → statistical MT → neural MT →
  large language models; what changed with each step.
- What LLMs are good at in linguistic work: bulk drafting of
  equivalents, suggesting paraphrases, detecting register
  inconsistencies, generating example sentences.
- What LLMs are bad at (and require human editorial control for):
  rare terms, regional variants, register-sensitive choices,
  fictional citations, "hallucinated" entries that look plausible
  but are not attested in real usage.
- *Prompt engineering* as an applied-linguistics task: explicit
  specification of source/target languages, register, normalisation
  rules, expected output format; iterative refinement of prompts
  (link directly to the three-stage prompt evolution in the
  practical part).
- The role of the linguist as the editor of AI output: deciding
  what counts as the same word (case, diacritics, partial match),
  resolving regional and register conflicts, verifying terminology
  against authoritative sources.
- Ethical considerations: attribution, the boundary between
  AI-assisted and AI-generated work, the limits of automated
  translation in safety-critical domains (which automotive
  documentation is).

**Sources to cite**
- Карпіловська Є. А. *Прикладна лінгвістика* (sections on MT).
- Koehn P. *Neural Machine Translation.*
- Vaswani A. et al. *Attention Is All You Need.* (NeurIPS, 2017) —
  the transformer paper underlying modern LLMs.
- Brown T. et al. *Language Models are Few-Shot Learners.*
  (NeurIPS, 2020) — GPT-3.
- Bender E. M. et al. *On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots.*
  (FAccT, 2021) — on the limits of LLMs.

## Conclusions to the theoretical part (≈ 0.5–1 page)

Short summary of the three theses the chapters establish:

1. The automobile industry forms a coherent terminological field with
   its own internal subdomains and a long history of cross-language
   exchange, which makes it well suited to a trilingual glossary
   project.
2. Modern lexicography is digital, and digital lexicography requires
   linguistic decisions (normalisation, search behaviour, error
   handling) that have no analogue in print.
3. Generative AI is a productive but not autonomous partner in
   lexicographic work: it accelerates drafting, but the role of the
   linguist as editor and as the author of the prompt remains
   indispensable.

These three conclusions motivate the practical part of the work,
described in the next chapter.
